It comes down to one word: **real**. StoryVoice gives you a machine's version of your customer's story. We give you your customer, on camera, in their own words. If you want ten written drafts a year on a small budget, StoryVoice. If you want proof a doubtful VP can't wave away, that's me.

## What StoryVoice is genuinely good at

I'm not going to pretend this is a bad tool. It isn't.

The scheduling problem is real, and StoryVoice solves it cleanly. Getting a busy customer to commit to a 30-minute booked call is often the single hardest part of the whole job. I know this because I do it for a living. StoryVoice sidesteps it entirely. You send a link, your customer talks to a voice AI whenever they've got five minutes, and there's no calendar tetris, no rescheduling, no "can we push to next week" three times running.

It's fast. One to three days from link to draft. Nothing done by a human comes close to that, mine included.

It's cheap. A free tier for grabbing testimonials at events, and paid plans that land well under $5,000 a year whichever one you pick. If you're a marketer with a target of ten-plus case studies this year and a budget that assumes you'll write most of them yourself, that maths works. Building volume from a standing start, where right now you have zero proof on the site, it's a genuinely sensible way to get moving.

And the drafts aren't garbage. It pulls quotes, it grabs the metrics your customer mentions, it structures the thing. As a first draft to edit, it saves you the blank page. For a certain buyer, that's exactly the job.

So if you're that buyer, cheap volume you'll finish yourself, honestly, go and use it. I mean that. The rest of this post is for the founder who wants something StoryVoice structurally can't give them.

## Where the Proof Kit wins: the interview is real

Here's the thing an AI voice interview can't do, and it's the whole ballgame.

It can't be there. It can't read that your customer just lit up talking about the thing that actually changed for them, and lean in, and say "wait, go back to that, tell me more." It can't hear the offhand line at minute nineteen that turns out to be the best quote in the whole piece. It can't tell when someone's giving you the polite rehearsed version versus the real one, and gently push past it. A five-minute scripted exchange with a bot gets you the surface. The good stuff lives underneath, and you get it by being a human who's genuinely curious in the room.

That's the difference between a draft and a story.

And then there's the format gap, which is bigger than it sounds. StoryVoice gives you words. That's it. No video case study, no video testimonial. In 2026 that's a problem, because video is the one proof asset that can't be faked. Anyone can type a glowing paragraph and stick a customer's name under it. Your buyer knows this. A real person, on camera, saying the thing out loud, is proof of a completely different order. It's the asset a sceptic can't argue with.

For [$2,500](/pricing/), one recorded conversation with your happiest customer becomes the whole set. A video case study (the full story on camera). A written case study built to get found in search and quoted by the AI tools your buyer now asks first. A short video testimonial for the homepage. A written pull-quote. A branded social pack cut from the same footage. You make one warm intro. Everything after that, the booking, the interview, the edit, is ours. Your name stays on the relationship the whole way through.

With StoryVoice, you send a link and then you're the editor. With us, you make an intro and then you're done.

## "But we're a fraction of the price and you get it in a day"

That's the pitch, and I'll concede both halves without a fight. StoryVoice is cheaper than me. StoryVoice is faster than me. A day versus a fortnight, a few thousand a year versus $2,500 a study. If price and speed are the only two axes you care about, they win. Genuinely.

But price and speed aren't why you're reading a page about case studies. You're reading it because you need proof that works. And here's where I'd gently point out something: if you've been burned before, the thing that burned you was probably cheap-and-fast in the first place.

I hear this constantly. Someone paid a freelancer £300 for a case study that reads like every other case study on the internet, or they ran their notes through ChatGPT and got something bland and weightless, and it sat on the site doing nothing. Cheap-and-AI is the trap, not the escape. A machine-drafted version of your customer's story is the exact "this feels generic, this doesn't feel real" thing that made your last attempt fall flat.

And the buyer has changed. 94% of B2B buyers now use LLMs somewhere in their buying process (that's 6sense's number), and the same buyers who use AI constantly are the quickest to spot it and trust it least when it's supposed to be a real human vouching for you. There's a study from Digital Applied showing pure AI content ranks around 23% lower on average in Google, and wider still on competitive queries. So the machine draft is weaker on the two things you actually wanted, credibility with a human and visibility in search, at once.

A case study has one job. Be the asset that survives scrutiny. AI output invites scrutiny. A real recorded human, on camera, ends it. I dug into this whole AI-and-DIY tier in more depth in the [case study pricing guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-b2b-case-study-cost/) if you want the longer version.
