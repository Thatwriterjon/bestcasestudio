**StoryVoice is best for cheap, fast volume you edit yourself. Best Case Studio is best for a founder who needs a real customer, on the record, that a buying committee can check.**

Here's the quick version. [**StoryVoice**](https://storyvoice.io/) is an AI tool that turns a five-minute voice interview into a written case study draft, cheap and fast, that you then edit and publish yourself. **Best Case Studio** sends a real human to run a real recorded interview and writes the story for you, written for $2,500 or written plus video for $5,000. It comes down to one word: **real**. StoryVoice gives you a machine's version of your customer's story. We give you your customer, on the record, in their own words. If you want ten written drafts a year on a small budget, StoryVoice. If you want proof a doubtful buying committee can't wave away, that's us.

I run [Best Case Studio](/), so you know which side I'm on before you read a word. Everything I say about StoryVoice below comes from their own site and links to it.

## What StoryVoice is genuinely good at

I'm not going to pretend this is a bad tool. It isn't.

The scheduling problem is real, and StoryVoice solves it cleanly. Getting a busy customer to commit to a booked call is often the hardest part of the whole job. StoryVoice sidesteps it: you send a link, your customer talks to a voice AI whenever they've got five minutes, no calendar tetris.

It's fast and it's cheap. A free trial where you interview yourself, then $39 a story (less in packs) or $249 a month for 50 on their own [pricing page](https://storyvoice.io/pricing), so you land well under $5,000 a year whichever way you buy. If you're building volume from a standing start, that maths works.

And the drafts aren't garbage. It pulls quotes, grabs the metrics your customer mentions, and structures the thing. As a first draft to edit, it saves you the blank page.

So if you're that buyer, cheap volume you'll finish yourself, honestly, go and use it. The rest of this post is for the founder who needs something StoryVoice structurally can't give them: a real, checkable customer.

## Where we win: the interview is real, and it's done for you

Here's the thing an AI voice interview can't do, and it's the whole ballgame.

It can't be there. It can't read that your customer just lit up talking about the thing that actually changed for them, and lean in, and say "wait, go back to that, tell me more." It can't hear the offhand line at minute nineteen that turns out to be the best quote in the whole piece. A five-minute scripted exchange with a bot gets you the surface. The good stuff lives underneath, and you get it by being a human who's genuinely curious in the room.

That's the difference between a draft and a story a buying committee can check.

And then there's the format gap. StoryVoice gives you words. That's it. No video. In 2026 that's a problem, because video is the one proof asset that's hardest to fake. For $5,000, one recorded conversation with your happiest customer becomes a written case study and a customer video, cut into every format your buyer checks. You make one warm intro. Jessie handles the ask, Jon runs the interview, and the relationship stays yours the whole way through.

With StoryVoice, you send a link and then you're the editor. With us, you make an intro and then you're done.

## "But we're a fraction of the price and you get it in a day"

That's the pitch, and I'll concede it without a fight. StoryVoice is cheaper than us. StoryVoice is faster than us. If price and speed are the only two axes you care about, they win. Genuinely.

But price and speed aren't why you're reading a page about case studies. You're reading it because you need proof that works when someone on a buying committee asks "why them?" And here's where I'd gently point out something: if you've been burned before, the thing that burned you was probably cheap-and-fast in the first place.

The buyer has changed, too. 94% of B2B buyers now use LLMs somewhere in their buying process ([6sense's number](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/)), and the same buyers who use AI constantly are the quickest to spot it and trust it least when it's supposed to be a real human vouching for you. There's a [study from Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-generated-vs-human-content-16-month-google-ranking-study) showing pure AI content ranks around 23% lower on average in Google, wider still on competitive queries.

A case study has one job: survive scrutiny. AI output invites scrutiny. A real recorded human ends it.
