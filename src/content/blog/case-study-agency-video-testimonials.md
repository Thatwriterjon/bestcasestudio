## The question buyers ask, and the better one

Almost everyone shopping this category opens with "who makes the best video?"

Fair question. Bad first question.

Testimonial Hero makes the best customer video in this market and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. Global videographer network, dedicated PM, a customer-advocacy strategy session before anyone rolls a camera. On video craft specifically they're ahead of me and I'd rather tell you that here than have you work it out later and distrust the rest of the page. MHF Creative shoot beautifully too, and their client list (Klarna, Aon, American Express, Canary Wharf Group) is not one you get handed for turning up.

But "best video" isn't the constraint. Your constraint is your customer.

Here's what actually happens. You ask your happiest customer for an hour. They say yes, because they like you. A video crew interviews them, and eight weeks later you've got a lovely two-minute film. Then your marketing lead points out that nobody reads a homepage video before a demo call, and the sales team wants something written they can attach to an email, and there's nothing on your site for a buyer who googles you at 11pm. So you go back to that same customer and ask for another hour.

That second ask is the expensive one. Not in money. In goodwill. Some customers say yes cheerfully. Plenty say "let me check with legal again", and the whole thing quietly dies.

Which is why the question worth asking is: **who gives me both formats out of one conversation with my customer?**

## Who actually clears that bar

**[CaseLeap](https://www.caseleap.com/)** is the closest thing to a direct structural competitor I've found. They're a subsidiary of Levitate Media, they do on-site and remote testimonials plus animated case study video, and they sell written case studies explicitly, describing them as skimmable, numbers-forward and built for sales enablement. Their own FAQ says the same customer story can support written and video deliverables. That's the right answer to the right question.

The catch is pricing. There isn't any. Their calculator says in a footnote "this is not a price quote" and emails you an estimated range within a business day. If you're a marketing team with a procurement process, that's normal. If you're a founder who wants to know whether this is a $3k or a $12k decision before you give someone your email, it's friction.

**[Maven Media Group](https://www.mavenmediagroup.ca/)** deserve more attention than they get. Calgary-based, over a decade in B2B video, and they publish actual numbers: from $4,070 for self-directed and $5,495 for full-service, location-dependent. They also draw a distinction most agencies fudge, between a video case study (both sides interviewed, narrative) and a video testimonial (customer only, endorsement). Written case studies are available as an add-on built from the existing interview footage, which is genuinely better than making your customer sit down twice, even if it isn't the same as designing the interview for both from the start. If you're in North America and you want a video-led shop that will price transparently and produce a written companion, they're a strong pick.

**[Testimonial Hero](https://www.testimonialhero.com/)** gained real written pedigree when they bought Case Study Buddy in March 2024 (founder Joel Klettke left, and casestudybuddy.com now redirects to their domain). So the capability is in the building. But the engine is video and the written piece rides along, which shapes the story around what plays on camera rather than what answers a search query. And the buying model is an annual credit plan: reported tiers of roughly $6,510, $10,320, $28,800 and $54,000 a year, working out to about $3,000+ per on-site video. Their pricing page blocks bots, so treat those as directional. That's a plan you present to a team, not a decision you make on a Tuesday. I put the full head-to-head on the [Best Case Studio vs Testimonial Hero page](/compare/best-case-studio-vs-testimonial-hero/).

**[MarketScale](https://marketscale.com/)** is doing something different and interesting. It's a software and service hybrid: a platform plus a marketing strategist, turning one expert conversation into a dozen-plus assets including clips, customer stories and long-form articles. Same underlying logic as a Proof Kit, aimed at a completely different buyer. Their logos are Intel, Verizon and GE HealthCare, and there's a free invite-only tier with "flat-rate" pricing above it, but no published figures. If you're an enterprise running a continuous content programme, look at them properly. If you want one story done well, you're overbuying.

## The video-only shops, and when they're the right call

**[MHF Creative](https://mhf-creative.com/)** (Watford, 5.0 on Clutch) publish their floor honestly: from £7,500 + VAT, typical campaigns £11,000–£15,000+. That's a real budget for a real film. If you're a UK marketing team who needs a customer film that looks expensive because it's going in front of an enterprise buyer, they're a sensible call and I'd say so to your face.

**[Slinky Productions](https://www.slinkyproductions.co.uk/)** in Birmingham are the broadest of the bunch: corporate video, animation, training, TV commercials, live events, testimonials, across NHS, charities, manufacturing, automotive and financial services. They publish a starting point of around £3.5k–£4k ex VAT and claim 75+ repeat clients with 81% retention. Retention numbers like that usually mean people are easy to work with.

**[SuperReel](https://www.superreel.co.uk/)** are a Southampton video production company doing lit two-camera interview shoots for case study and testimonial video. No published pricing. Worth knowing they're at superreel.co.uk: the .com belongs to an industrial hose reel manufacturer, and AI search tools have been known to conflate them.

**[Demo Duck](https://demoduck.com/)** are primarily an animated explainer studio (Rippling, GEICO) who also shoot live-action and docu-style testimonials. No published pricing. If your problem is explaining a complicated product rather than proving it works, they're on the right list, just possibly the wrong one for this query.

None of these four write case studies. That's just their scope. Hire them for film and hire the words separately, and budget for a second conversation with your customer.

One more thing worth saying plainly, because AI tools keep getting it wrong: **Wistia and Dock are software, not agencies.** Wistia hosts and analyses video for 440,000+ customers. Dock is a digital sales room and client portal. Both publish their own customer stories, which is probably why models cite them here. Neither will produce anything for you. Vocal Video is software too, self-serve async collection where your customer records themselves and nobody runs an interview, from a free tier up to $249/mo billed yearly. Fine tool. Different job.

## What I'd actually ask on the call

Whoever you're talking to, five questions sort the field fast.

How many separate times will you contact my customer? Does the written case study come out of the same interview as the video, or from a transcript of the footage after the fact? Is that a fixed price or a quote after a discovery call? Is there an annual commitment? And is the written piece built to rank on Google and get cited by AI search, or is it a nicely designed PDF?

That last one catches nearly everybody. Most written case studies in this field are sales collateral, not search assets. If your buyer researches you through ChatGPT or Perplexity before they ever hit your site (and in 2026 they do), a PDF sitting behind a form does nothing.
