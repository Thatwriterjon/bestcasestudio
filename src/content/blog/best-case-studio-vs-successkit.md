## What SuccessKit is genuinely good at

Let me get the honest part out of the way, because it's substantial.

[SuccessKit](https://successkit.io/) is properly done-for-you. They schedule the call, run the interview, write the study, design it, and chase your customer for approval. The G2 reviews aren't vague either. People keep saying the same thing: it took almost no effort on their end. That's a real deliverable in itself. Anyone who's tried to get a case study over the line internally knows the killer isn't the writing, it's the fifteen small tasks nobody has time to own. SuccessKit owns them.

Design is included, not an upsell. You get a custom-branded template, the PDF, and the source files, with revisions included. That's a clean deal.

Then there's the sales-enablement layer, which is the bit that actually sets them apart. A content library with tagging that plugs into your sales stack, so your sales team can find the right case study for the right deal instead of digging through a shared drive from 2023. If you're a marketing team producing case studies that a sales team then has to use, that's a real problem solved. Most case study shops hand you a PDF and wave goodbye. SuccessKit thinks about what happens after.

And the pricing is public. $1,750+ for a case study, $2,000+ for a short video testimonial, $3,000+ for a white paper. Bulk discounts if you order several. After months of researching this market and hitting "book a call to discuss pricing" on half the sites I visited, I have a lot of time for a company that just tells you the number.

So this isn't a hit piece. If you're a sales or marketing team that wants a low-effort case study machine feeding a searchable library, SuccessKit is a strong, sensible choice.

## Where the Proof Kit wins: everything from one conversation

Here's the difference, and it's not about who writes a better sentence.

SuccessKit's $1,750 written case study is cheaper than my $2,500 Proof Kit. I'm not going to pretend otherwise. If all you want is words on a page, they'll do it well and do it for less. I'd be taking the piss to claim a price win on writing, so I won't.

The win is completeness per interview.

Your happy customer gives you one good hour. That hour is the hard-won bit: the intro, the scheduling, the goodwill you're spending to ask them to show up. The question is how many formats of proof you get out of that single hour. SuccessKit turns it into one asset, the written study. Want video too? That's a separate $2,000+ product. Want it cut for social? Another line. Each format is another purchase, and some of them mean another interview or another scheduling dance with a customer who's already done you a favour.

The Proof Kit takes that same hour and gives you back the lot. One recorded conversation becomes:

Same customer, same hour, one price. You make one warm intro and step back. We book it, run the interview, and cut everything from the footage. Fourteen days later it's all back with you, ready to publish. You keep the customer relationship the whole way through.

## "We manage everything and plug into your sales workflow"

That's the fair rebuttal from the SuccessKit side, and it's a good one. So let me answer it straight.

On the managing-everything part, we're level. The Proof Kit is fully done-for-you on the production side. You're not editing anything, writing anything, or chasing anyone. The only thing that stays with you is the customer relationship, and that's on purpose, because the intro and the credit should be yours, not your vendor's. Where we differ is speed and shape: 14 days versus several weeks, one fixed price versus a per-asset menu, and a full video case study in the box rather than a short clip you buy separately.

On the sales-workflow part, I'm going to concede it cleanly. We don't have a content library. We don't push assets into your sales tools. We don't do tagging or a CRM layer. If that's what you need, SuccessKit has something I simply don't offer, and I'm not going to bodge together a worse version to look complete.

But be honest with yourself about whether you need it. A CRM-connected case study library is a marketing-team-and-sales-team problem. It matters when you've got reps pulling different studies for different deals every week. A solo founder with one brilliant customer story doesn't have that problem. You have a different one: you need proof, in every format a buyer checks, without it eating your Q3. That's the founder I built the Proof Kit for.
