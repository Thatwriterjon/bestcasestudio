A B2B case study will cost you somewhere between **$0 and $15,000**. Here's the quick breakdown:

- **DIY / AI-generated:** $0–$500.
- **Freelance writer:** $1,000–$2,500 per case study.
- **Specialist boutique:** $1,500–$3,000 per case study.
- **Full-service content agency:** $5,000–$15,000+ (usually bundled in a monthly retainer).

That's the short answer. I run [Best Case Studio](/), so I'll be upfront about where I sit in this and where my biases are. But I've also worked with or compete against every tier on this list. I'm not guessing at the numbers.

## What does a freelance case study writer charge?

**$1,000–$2,500 per case study.**

At the lower end, you're getting someone who writes well but treats a case study like any other long-form content project. They'll take your brief, write something clean, and hand you a Google Doc. Done.

At the upper end ($2,000ish) you're probably getting someone with actual B2B chops who knows how to structure a case study rather than writing it like another blog post. That distinction matters *way more* than most people think.

But here's the thing about freelancers: the quality can vary *massively*. I've seen freelancer-written case studies that were genuinely fantastic: tight narrative, real quotes, specific results, etc. But I've also seen some that read like the writer googled "case study template" ten minutes before they started. You're betting on the individual, and there's no system backing them up.

What you won't get from most freelancers: SEO strategy, schema markup, any thought about AI citability, or guidance on how to publish it on your website. Most hand you a document and that's where their job ends.

(No shade here at all. Some of my best friends are freelancers)

So if you already know what you want, you've got the customer interview sorted, and you just need a skilled pair of hands to write it...a freelancer is the right call. But stress-test "skilled" before you commit, because there's nothing behind them except their own taste.

## What does a specialist boutique charge?

**$1,500–$3,000 per case study.**

*Full disclosure: this is where we sit. Best Case Studio's [Proof Kit is $2,500](/pricing/), so take everything in this section with a healthy pinch of salt.*

But here's why this tier exists.

A specialist does case studies all day. Not blog posts, not whitepapers, not ad copy that happens to include a case study on the side. Just case studies. Which means the interview framework, the way the headings are shaped, the schema markup on the page, etc. is usually tried and tested.

The prices bounce around inside the range. [Uplift Content](https://www.upliftcontent.com/) doesn't publish a number. You get a quote after a discovery call, and they lean towards ongoing clients. [SuccessKit](https://successkit.io/) is refreshingly public: $1,750+ for a written case study, $2,000+ for a 60–90 second video testimonial, billed per asset. [Concurate](https://concurate.com/) runs tiers reportedly around $1,500–$2,500. [Ace Case Studies](https://www.acecasestudies.com/) by Jody Williams is a solo specialist with impressive creds (Oracle, Gorgias), priced on request.

The practical difference from a freelancer is everything that happens *around* the writing. A specialist will schedule and run the customer interview, decide which story to tell (not every happy customer makes a good case study), structure the page for search and AI, and hand you an implementation guide for whatever CMS you're running. Not a generic PDF. And maybe even instructions for *your* stack (that's what we do; here are [some examples](/examples/)).

One thing to watch at this tier: almost all of it is written-only. The video, if it exists at all, is a bolt-on you scope and pay for separately. Which is where the real cost hides. Buy your proof à la carte from specialists — the written study from a writer ($1,750+), the video from a video shop ($3,000+), a video testimonial as its own line ($2,000+), plus the pull-quote and social — and you're looking at $7,000–$10,000+ across three or four vendors. Our $2,500 covers the whole set (video *and* written case study, video *and* written testimonial, and a social pack) from one interview. That's not a price play on the writing. SuccessKit and Concurate will match or undercut us on words alone. It's a completeness play. Same conversation, every format, one bill.

The downside of this tier? You're trusting a small operation (sometimes just one or two people) to deliver. If they get overwhelmed, there's no bench. That's a real concern.

## What does a full-service content agency charge?

**$5,000–$15,000+ per case study.** Usually as part of a monthly retainer.

At this tier, you're not buying a case study. You're buying a content programme. Agencies like [Grow & Convert](https://www.growandconvert.com/) ($9–10k/month), [Omniscient Digital](https://beomniscient.com/) ($10k/month), or [Ten Speed](https://www.tenspeed.io/) sell multi-month packages that include blogs, landing pages, SEO strategy, and (somewhere in the mix) case studies.

Break down a $10k/month retainer and the effective cost per case study lands somewhere between $5,000 and $15,000, depending on how many other deliverables are eating into that budget.

I have two problems with this tier for case studies specifically.

**First:** the person who writes your case study is almost never the strategist you met on the sales call. They're a contractor. Sometimes literally a freelancer on the agency's bench, being billed out at 3x their rate. So you're paying agency money for freelancer output with an account manager in between.

**Second:** case studies aren't the agency's *thing*. They're a line item or upsell. The blog posts get the strategic attention. The SEO audits get the senior person. Your case study gets written by whoever's free that week, using whatever template they've had since 2021.

And I'm speaking from experience here. The first case study I ever wrote was for a SaaS marketing agency (not naming names) who dropped it on my desk with very little guidance. I had no idea what I was doing, so guessed my way through it. Was it an awful case study? Not at all. But was it worth the $4,500 they were billed for it? Absolutely fucking not. 

There are exceptions, of course. Ten Speed, for one, actually productises case studies as a specific offering alongside their SEO work. But they're the outlier.

If you're already running a content programme and want case studies folded into it...fine. But if all you want is three solid case studies? You're paying for project managers, Slack channels, and kickoff decks that add nothing to the words on the page.

## What about writing it yourself (or using AI)?

**$0–$500.** Cheapest on paper. Let me tell you how this usually goes.

**Phase one:** someone at the company decides case studies are a priority. It goes on the roadmap. Marketing will handle it. Then Q2 gets busy. Then the customer who agreed to participate goes on holiday. Then someone leaves the company and nobody picks it up. Six months later it still hasn't shipped. I've seen this play out at *so many* B2B SaaS companies that I'd call it the default outcome, not the exception.

This is something I've heard a lot since we launched Best Case Studio. It's been on their to-do list for months or even years, but just never get around to doing it.

**Phase two (which is increasingly common):** someone suggests using ChatGPT. And here's the thing about AI-generated case studies: they *look* decent. They *look* like a proper case study.

But it's almost always a generic story, with no personality, no emotion, and no hook. 

The problem is that AI can't interview your customer. That means no real quotes, no numbers the customer actually verified, no story that a buyer reads and thinks "that sounds like my problem." AI either invents metrics (which is a credibility and legal problem) or falls back on filler like "significant improvement in efficiency." Neither of those convinces anyone to spend money.

There's the ranking issue too. Pure AI content performs measurably worse on competitive search queries ([roughly 23% lower, according to Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/ai-generated-vs-human-content-16-month-google-ranking-study)). Google's helpful content system is built to spot and demote pages that exist to game search rather than help a person.

And then there's the irony nobody talks about: a case study is supposed to prove that real humans use your product and get real results. If the case study *itself* was clearly written by a machine…what exactly are you proving?

Templates are a different thing. HubSpot, Notion, whoever. They'll give you a structure. That solves the easy 10%. It does nothing for the hard 90%: getting the customer on the phone, asking the right questions, and turning a rambling 30-minute conversation into something a sceptical VP reads in 45 seconds and decides to book a demo.

## What are you actually paying for?

Most pricing guides stop at the table. Here's the price, pick one, good luck.

But the reason a case study can cost $1,000 or $10,000 has nothing to do with word count. Nobody's charging by the paragraph. The price reflects how much of the *process* you're outsourcing. And most of the process is invisible to the buyer.

Think of it in three phases.

**Before writing.** Someone has to figure out which customer to feature and what story to tell. Not every happy customer makes a good case study. You want a clear before-and-after, a specific metric, and a situation that resembles the buyer you're trying to reach. Then someone has to run an interview that pulls out *quotable, specific* proof. Not "tell us about your experience" (which gets you generic praise), but pointed questions about the exact problem, the decision, and the numbers.

**The writing.** Turning a rambling interview into something a busy buyer will actually read. And not just the buyer, but also the bots that inform Google, AI chatbots, etc. The writer should include question-shaped headings that match real search queries and an opening "citation block" (the first 120 words structured as a complete, self-contained answer) so that when someone asks ChatGPT "which sales tax tool should I use?" or "what's the best LinkedIn engagement platform?", there's a chunk for AI to quote directly.

**After writing.** SEO metadata. JSON-LD schema markup, etc. This is what we call the 'machine layer' that Google and AI tools parse even though no human ever sees it. And Open Graph tags so it doesn't look rubbish when someone shares it on LinkedIn.

When we deliver case studies, we also include a step-by-step guide for publishing on whatever CMS you're running. It sucks to be handed a Google Doc with some best wishes, so we help you implement the case study properly.

A $1,000 freelancer handles the writing. A $2,500 specialist handles all three. And the good ones now harvest the same interview into video and social too, so you're not paying a separate vendor for each format. A $10,000 agency handles all three, plus a kickoff call, a status call, a retrospective call to discuss the calls, and some very well paid middle-management. 

## When does a cheap case study cost you more than an expensive one?

When nobody reads it.

I'm not being glib. Most B2B companies *already have* case studies. They're a PDF on the site somewhere. Sales attaches them to emails. Or they're just stuck on the website like they're another blog post. *Nobody*  can find them through search, and no AI tool can cite them. 

The money you spent producing that case study isn't the real cost. It's the deals you didn't close because your proof was invisible. [94% of buyers are using LLMs in their buying process](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/). So if your case study isn't findable during that research phase (especially through ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc), it's doing nothing for you.

**Quick maths:** a $2,500 case study that ranks for a buying-intent query and brings in even *two* qualified leads a year has paid for itself many times over. A $500 case study that never ranks, never gets cited, and only gets seen by people who already had the link? That cost you $500 plus every opportunity it failed to create.

## How do you choose the right option?

I'll keep this short and sweet...

**DIY** only works if you have a genuine writer in-house, a willing customer, and the *actual discipline* to ship it. Most teams don't. Not because they're lazy, but because case studies always lose the priority fight against product launches, campaigns, and whatever's on fire this week.

**A freelancer** makes sense if you've got the interview done (or can handle it yourself) and you know what the case study should say. You're buying a pair of writing hands, not a strategy. Fine if your team already has an opinion on what good proof looks like.

**A specialist** is where I'd point most growing B2B companies. You get the interview, the narrative, the technical optimisation, and proof that's built to actually generate pipeline rather than sit in a sales folder. The best of them hand you every format from one conversation, not just a written PDF. ([Go and look at a finished example](/examples/) if you want to see the difference.) Sure, I'm biased. But the pricing data across the market puts it squarely in the sweet spot between "doing it all yourself" and "signing a retainer you don't need."

**An agency** is the right call if you already run a content programme and want case studies as *part* of the package. Just make sure they have a real case study methodology and aren't treating yours as a bolt-on afterthought with a template from three years ago.
