**Content agencies are best for a company that wants case studies folded into a wider content and SEO programme. Best Case Studio is best for a founder who wants one thing, proof, done brilliantly and nothing else.**

If you already run an ongoing content and SEO programme and you want case studies quietly folded into it, a broad agency is a sensible call. [Animalz](https://www.animalz.co/), [Brafton](https://www.brafton.com/), [Siege Media](https://www.siegemedia.com/) and [The Blogsmith](https://www.theblogsmith.com/) are genuinely good at content. But if the one job you actually need done is an answer to "why them?", and you want it done brilliantly rather than adequately, that's a different shopping trip. What sets Best Case Studio apart here is **focus**. Case studies are all we do. At an agency, your case study is one line on a very long menu.

I run [Best Case Studio](/), so you should know where my bias sits before you read another word. Everything I say about the agencies below comes from their own websites. Agency pricing for a single case study isn't published anywhere I could verify, so I've flagged that honestly rather than guess.

## What a content agency is actually good at

Let me concede the real stuff, because it matters.

These agencies can write. Animalz built its name on premium B2B SaaS content and has worked with the likes of SupportLogic and Frontify. The Blogsmith does SEO-driven content with proper interviews and design, and has names like Klaviyo and Clearscope on its roster. Brafton and Siege Media run serious content operations with real distribution muscle behind them. This is not a "freelancer who googled a template" situation. The bench is deep and the craft is there.

The bigger point in their favour: if you're already running a content programme, folding case studies into it is properly convenient. One partner. One invoice. One team who already knows your product, your voice and your buyer.

I'm not going to talk you out of that. If it describes you, some of you reading this should stop here and go brief your agency. Honestly.

## Where we win: it's the only thing we do

Here's the catch with "we do everything." When a company does everything, no single thing is the thing.

At a broad agency, case studies are a service line. They sit below the blog engine and the SEO retainer on the priority list. The senior strategist you met on the sales call is running the content calendar. Your case study gets written by whoever's free that week, often off a template that's been kicking around since 2021.

I know this because it's how I started. The first case study I ever wrote was for a SaaS marketing agency that dropped it on my desk with almost no guidance. I had no framework, no interview training, no idea what a good one even looked like. I guessed my way through it. That's the model in one story: agency money, guessed-my-way-through output, because the case study was never anyone's main job.

This is our main job. The only one. That focus buys you three things an agency structurally can't match on this specific deliverable.

First, **the ask is handled for you**. You tell us which customer. Jessie makes the ask, books the call and handles approvals, so nobody on your team has to chase your own customer.

Second, **a fixed price for the one job that matters**. No retainer. No "let's scope it." $2,500 written, $5,000 written plus video, known before you book.

Third, **speed**. 14 days from the interview, because there's no content calendar your case study is queuing behind.

## "But we want one partner for everything"

This is the objection, and it's a fair one. The agency pitch is "we do everything, so you only manage one relationship." Tidy. Appealing. And it's exactly the weakness on proof specifically.

Breadth is a real strength for a rolling content operation. It's a liability for the one deliverable that has to carry the most weight when a buying committee asks "why them?". Specialists beat generalists on the thing they specialise in. You wouldn't hire your GP to do your knee surgery, even though your GP is a perfectly good doctor. The agency is your GP. Proof is the knee surgery. We do knees.

## Choose a content agency if

You're already running, or about to run, an ongoing content and SEO programme, and you have someone in-house to manage the relationship. If case studies are one deliverable inside a broader strategy and you'd rather not add another vendor, a good agency is honestly the right home for them. Just make sure they treat case studies as a real methodology and not a bolt-on, and ask directly who'll be writing yours.

## Choose Best Case Studio if

Proof is the job. You don't have a content machine to plug this into, you don't want a retainer, and you want the ask, the interview and the write-up handled by people who do nothing else.
