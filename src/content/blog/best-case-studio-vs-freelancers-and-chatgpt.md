**A freelancer or ChatGPT is best if you've already got the customer and the interview, and just need the writing done. Best Case Studio is best if you want the whole job, the ask, the interview and the write-up, done for you.**

The honest version: if you've already interviewed your customer and you just need a skilled writer, a good freelancer is a fine call and a lot cheaper than us. If you want to type "write me a case study" into ChatGPT and ship whatever comes out, please don't. The thing that separates a case study that convinces from content that gets ignored is that it's **real**: a real customer, on the record. AI can't give you that, and a freelancer only gives you the writing half of it.

I've also *been* the cheap option. I spent years as a freelance writer taking case study briefs off Upwork and agencies. So when I talk about freelancers, I'm not sneering from a great height. I know exactly how good that route can be and exactly where it falls over.

## What ChatGPT and a good freelancer are genuinely good at

Let me concede the obvious, because it's real.

They're cheaper. They're faster. And you keep full control the whole way through. For a freelancer specifically, the ceiling is higher than people give it credit for. A genuinely good freelance writer can produce a case study that's every bit as sharp as anything a studio ships. The best of them are excellent, and they charge a fraction of what a studio does.

The catch with freelancers is *variance*. A good one is genuinely excellent, but you're betting on the individual, and there's no system behind them. When it's good it's very good. When it's bad you've lost your money and your time, and you often can't tell which one you've bought until it lands.

ChatGPT is a different animal, and I want to be fair about what it's actually good at too. It's a brilliant drafting tool. If you've done the interview yourself and you've got a transcript full of real quotes, feeding that to an AI to help shape a first draft is completely legitimate. That's using it as a tool. The problem is what happens when people use it as the *whole process*.

## Where we win: the whole job, done for you, and real

Here's the thing a freelancer and AI both leave on your plate: finding the customer, making the ask, and running the interview.

With a freelancer, you do all of that, then hand over your notes or a transcript for them to write up. With ChatGPT, you do all of that and then the AI has no real quotes to work from unless you supply a transcript, and even then it can invent metrics or reach for filler like "streamlined their workflow and saw significant improvement in efficiency." You've read a thousand of those sentences. So has your buying committee.

We start from the ask. Jessie gets the customer's yes. Jon runs the interview. One conversation becomes a [written case study, or written plus a customer video, social videos and a LinkedIn carousel](/examples/saasydb-leadforce-solutions/). 2026 buyers have their radar up, too. Nine in ten of them ([94%, per 6sense](https://6sense.com/science-of-b2b/buyer-experience-report-2025/)) now lean on LLMs somewhere in their buying process, which means they're staring at AI-generated text all day, and they've gotten very good at spotting it. ## "I'll just use ChatGPT" / "a freelancer's way cheaper"

You will, and it is. Both true. I'm not going to pretend otherwise.

The question isn't whether the cheap route is cheaper. It obviously is. The question is what your proof is *for*. It exists to convince a sceptical buying committee that's about to spend real money and is looking for a reason to believe you or a reason to bail. If that buyer reads your case study and thinks "this feels generic" or "did a bot write this?", it's done the opposite of its job.

## Choose a freelancer if

You've done the interview (or you'll do it yourself), you know what a good case study should say, and you just need skilled writing hands. Ask for samples, and read them like your buyer would.

## Choose ChatGPT if

You want to draft, structure, or tidy your own writing, or get unstuck. Just don't let it be the finished, published thing, and never let it invent a number.
