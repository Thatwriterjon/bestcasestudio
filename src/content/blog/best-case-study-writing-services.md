## How did I choose who to include?

Three rules. They must offer done-for-you B2B case study writing (not templates, not just video, not academic essay mills). They must have verifiable clients or a portfolio. And pricing must be either published or reliably estimated — I'm not listing anyone if I'd have to guess the number.

One thing worth flagging: if you google "case study writing service" without a qualifier, half the results are sites like Academized and PapersOwl selling essays to university students. Totally different buyer. Everything below is B2B-specific.

## Who are the best case study writing services for B2B SaaS?

They charge from $750 per case study, plus a $250–$500 onboarding fee for new clients. You get background research, a recorded customer interview with customised questions, the case study, and two rounds of revisions. Social posts, video interviews, sales slides, and one-pagers are available as extras. 5% loyalty discount if you book six or more pieces.

Their client list is the real thing. ClickUp, Okta, WalkMe, LeanData, ON24. Those aren't logos you get handed for writing a blog post.

### Testimonial Hero (+ what happened to Case Study Buddy)

This entry is really two stories.

**Case Study Buddy** was *the* name in written B2B case studies. Joel Klettke built it, and for a while it was the first service most SaaS marketers thought of. Then in March 2024, Testimonial Hero acquired them. Joel left. The casestudybuddy.com domain now redirects to testimonialhero.com.

If you came here searching for Case Study Buddy, that's what happened. The standalone written case study service doesn't exist anymore.

[Testimonial Hero](https://www.testimonialhero.com/) is a video testimonial company. Written case studies exist inside their offering, but they're the sidecar, not the engine. A written-only case study costs $3,800 (remote). Video + written starts at $6,000 (remote) and $11,600+ for onsite shoots. Turnaround is roughly 45 days.

### Compose.ly

[Compose.ly](https://www.compose.ly/) is a managed content marketplace — blogs, articles, ebooks, and case studies through a vetted writer network. Case studies are on their à la carte menu but pricing isn't published — you'll need a quote. Turnaround is listed at 5–7 days, which is fast for a done-for-you service.

The appeal is convenience. If you're already using Compose.ly for blog posts, adding case studies through the same vendor saves you finding someone new. But you're getting a marketplace writer, not a named specialist who does case studies all day. The writer who handles your case study might also be writing product descriptions for other clients that week. No GEO, no structured data, no implementation guidance.

That is a *lot* of deliverables for $649. No other service on this list bundles PDF design and social content at that price.

The trade-off is specificity. Column isn't SaaS-specialised — they work across industries. The "search engine optimised" claim in their FAQs means basic SEO, not GEO or AI-citation engineering. No notable SaaS logos. At that price point, you're getting a functional case study, not a strategic one. It'll work for sales enablement. It won't rank for a competitive query or get quoted by an AI.

Best fit: early-stage companies on a tight budget that need *a* case study and will handle distribution themselves.

The one I'd pay most attention to is **Ten Speed**. They actually productise case studies as a specific offering alongside their SEO work, and they market AI-search visibility explicitly. Most other agencies treat case studies as a bolt-on. Ten Speed seems to take them seriously.

But the thing about agencies and case studies — and I wrote about this in the pricing guide too — is that the person who writes your case study is almost never the strategist you met on the sales call. It's a contractor. Sometimes literally a freelancer being billed out at three times their rate. So you're paying agency money for freelancer output with an account manager in between.

## What about freelancers and DIY?

Covered in detail in the [pricing guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-b2b-case-study-cost/), so I'll keep it short.

Freelancers charge $1,000–$2,500. Quality varies *massively*. No SEO, no GEO, no structured data, no CMS guidance. You're betting on the individual.

DIY costs $0–$500 and almost never ships. The case study goes on the to-do list, Q2 gets busy, the willing customer goes on holiday, and six months later it still hasn't happened. I hear this from nearly every company that reaches out to us.

## Which option is right for you?

**You want your case study to actually show up in search and AI answers.** Hire a specialist. Uplift if you want affordability and a proven SaaS team. Best Case Studio if you want the AI-citation layer built in. Ace if you want enterprise-level craft and aren't fussed about search.

**You're already running a content programme.** Fold case studies into your agency retainer — but ask if they have a dedicated case study process. Ten Speed is the one to watch.

**You need a case study on a tight budget.** Column at $649. Or a good freelancer at $1,000–$1,500. Either way, you're handling distribution.

**You want video testimonials with written as a supplement.** Testimonial Hero. Budget $6,000+ for remote video + written.

## One thing nobody else is talking about (yet)

Every content agency now claims some version of GEO or AEO. It's the buzzword of 2026. But almost all of them are applying it to *blog posts*.

Case studies are different. They're the highest-trust, highest-intent content a B2B company publishes. When a buyer asks ChatGPT "which sales tax automation tools have the best customer results?" or "has anyone used [your product] successfully?" — the answer *should* come from a case study. Not a blog post.

Yet almost no case study service actually engineers for that moment. Uplift offers AEO for content generally, which is more than most — but not specifically for case studies. Everyone else? Silent. The case study industry is still producing PDFs and Google Docs while the way buyers research is changing underneath them.
