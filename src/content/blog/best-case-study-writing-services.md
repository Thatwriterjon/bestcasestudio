One axis that's missing from most comparison tables, including this one: whether the written case study is actually built to rank on Google and get cited by AI. Almost none of these are. Mine is, and I'll come back to why that matters at the end.

## How did I choose who to include?

Three rules. They must offer done-for-you B2B case studies (not templates, not just video, not academic essay mills). They must have verifiable clients or a portfolio. And pricing must be either published or reliably estimated. Where a service hides the number behind "book a call", I'll say so rather than invent one.

One thing worth flagging: if you google "case study writing service" without a qualifier, half the results are sites like Academized and PapersOwl selling essays to university students. Totally different buyer. Everything below is B2B-specific.

## Who are the best case study services for B2B SaaS?

### Uplift Content

[Uplift Content](https://www.upliftcontent.com/) is probably the most established name in B2B SaaS case study writing right now. Boutique team (not a solo operator, not a massive agency), focused on SaaS content: case studies alongside blog posts, ebooks, and white papers.

They don't publish a price. You get a fixed quote after a discovery call, plus a scope-of-work document, and they lean towards ongoing relationships rather than one-off jobs (they've said they take a limited number of new ongoing clients a year). Background research, a recorded customer interview with customised questions, the written case study, and revisions are the core. Video and audio testimonials and case-study design are add-ons, not part of the base.

Their client list is the real thing. ClickUp, Okta, WalkMe, LeanData, ON24. Those aren't logos you get handed for writing a blog post.

Where it gets interesting is the format gap. Uplift is written-led. Video is an add-on you scope separately, there's no social pack as standard, and they work with SaaS only, so if you sell services you bounce off them. They do offer answer-engine optimisation as a service for content generally, which is more than most, but it isn't baked into the case study deliverable. If all you need is an excellent written case study from a proven SaaS writer, that's exactly what they are.

Best fit: SaaS marketing teams that want a reliable, ongoing writing partner who knows the space.

### SuccessKit

[SuccessKit](https://successkit.io/) is a fully-managed case study service with a sales-enablement layer bolted on. They schedule, interview, write, design, and secure your customer's approval, and reviewers consistently praise how little work it takes on your end. The output is a written case study with PDF and source files, a custom-branded template, and a content library with tagging plus a Salesforce integration, so your sales team can actually find and use the thing.

Pricing is refreshingly public: $1,750+ for a case study, $2,000+ for a 60–90 second video testimonial, $3,000+ for a white paper. All revisions included, you own the content, bulk discounts if you order several.

The catch is the same one that runs through this whole list. SuccessKit is written-led. The video is a short add-on, not a full video case study, and there's no social pack. Buy a case study *and* a video testimonial to get two formats and you're at $3,750 for two vendors' worth of proof, still with no video case study. Turnaround is around six weeks. It's built for sales and marketing teams who want a searchable library, not for a founder who wants one polished story done fast.

Best fit: sales and marketing teams that want a managed, low-effort case study machine that plugs into their CRM.

### Testimonial Hero (+ what happened to Case Study Buddy)

This entry is really two stories.

**Case Study Buddy** was *the* name in written B2B case studies. Joel Klettke built it, and for a while it was the first service most SaaS marketers thought of. Then in March 2024, Testimonial Hero acquired them. Joel left. The casestudybuddy.com domain now redirects to testimonialhero.com.

If you came here searching for Case Study Buddy, that's what happened. The standalone written service doesn't exist anymore.

(To learn more about that sale, you can [listen to my chat with him here](https://saasyasfuck.substack.com/p/ep-14-standing-out-in-the-crowded) from the SaaSy as Fuck podcast)

[Testimonial Hero](https://www.testimonialhero.com/) is a video testimonial company, and a genuinely good one. Written case studies live inside their offering now, but they're the sidecar, not the engine. The pricing model is worth understanding: it's credit-based *annual* tiers, starting around $6,510/yr and climbing to $28,800 and beyond, working out to roughly $3,000+ per video. Turnaround is a few weeks. Their exact numbers sit behind a sales call, so treat the figures as directional.

The production quality on their videos is the best in this list, no argument. If you're a funded marketing team that needs broadcast-grade video and can commit to an annual plan, they're the obvious call.

But that's the whole point of difference. It's an annual credit commitment aimed at marketing departments, not a fixed one-off for a founder. And because the written case study is a secondary output of a video-first process, the narrative gets shaped by what plays on camera rather than what ranks on a search results page. I don't try to out-video them. I win by giving a founder the complete set (video *and* written *and* social) from one interview, once, for one fixed price, with no annual credits.

Best fit: funded B2B teams that want the best video testimonials money can buy and can commit annually.

### StoryVoice

[StoryVoice](https://storyvoice.io/) is the newest shape of competitor: an AI tool. You send your customer a link, they talk to a voice AI for about five minutes, and it spits out a publish-ready written draft with quotes and metrics. You then edit and publish it yourself. Under $5k/year, a free tier for event testimonials, drafts in one to three days.

For volume, it's clever. If you want ten studies a year on a small budget and your customers are too busy for a scheduled interview, I can see the appeal.

But read the room. It's AI-generated, written-only, and *you* do the editing, so it isn't done-for-you. And it walks straight into the exact problem proof is meant to solve. Your buyer in 2026 fact-checks everything and trusts AI output least of all. A machine-spun draft of a customer story is the thing a sceptical VP smells from a mile off. The whole job of a case study is to be the one asset that survives scrutiny. An AI draft invites it.

Best fit: DIY teams chasing volume who'll happily edit the output themselves.

### Compose.ly

[Compose.ly](https://www.compose.ly/) is a managed content marketplace: blogs, articles, ebooks, and case studies through a vetted writer network. Case studies are on the à la carte menu but pricing isn't published, so you'll need a quote. Turnaround is listed at 5–7 days, fast for done-for-you.

The appeal is convenience. If you already use them for blogs, adding case studies through the same vendor saves you finding someone new. But you're getting a marketplace writer, not a named specialist who does case studies all day. The writer on your case study might be writing product descriptions for someone else that week. No video, no social, no structured data, no implementation guidance.

### Column

[Column](https://columncontent.com/) is the budget option. $649 (basic, 2–3 weeks) or $799 (deluxe, 1 week). Both include a customer interview, the written case study, PDF design, social posts, social graphics, and one round of copy editing.

That's a *lot* of deliverables for $649. No other service here bundles PDF design and social at that price.

The trade-off is specificity. Column isn't SaaS-specialised. They work across industries. The "search engine optimised" claim means basic SEO, not AI-citation engineering. No notable SaaS logos, and no video. At that price you're getting a functional written case study, not a strategic one. It'll work for sales enablement. It won't rank for a competitive query or get quoted by an AI.

Best fit: early-stage companies on a tight budget that need *a* written case study and will handle distribution themselves.

## What about full-service content agencies?

Agencies like [Grow & Convert](https://www.growandconvert.com/) (from $10k/month), [Omniscient Digital](https://beomniscient.com/) ($10k/month), [Ten Speed](https://www.tenspeed.io/), [Animalz](https://www.animalz.co/), and [Siege Media](https://www.siegemedia.com/) all produce case studies as one deliverable inside a broader content retainer.

Break down a $10k/month retainer and the effective cost per case study lands between $5,000 and $15,000, depending on what else is eating the budget. I wrote a full [pricing breakdown here](/blog/how-much-does-a-b2b-case-study-cost/) if you want the maths.

The one I'd pay most attention to is **Ten Speed**. They actually productise case studies as a specific offering alongside their SEO work, and they market AI-search visibility explicitly. Most other agencies treat case studies as a bolt-on. Ten Speed seems to take them seriously.

But the thing about agencies and case studies (and I wrote about this in the pricing guide too) is that the person who writes yours is almost never the strategist you met on the sales call. It's a contractor. Sometimes literally a freelancer billed out at three times their rate. So you're paying agency money for freelancer output with an account manager in between.

(I've been that freelancer btw, cluelessly guessing how to write a case study before I'd learned how)

If you're already running a content programme and want case studies folded in, fine. But if all you want is three solid case studies? You're paying for Slack channels and kickoff decks that add nothing to the words on the page. And it's still written-only.

## What about freelancers and DIY?

Covered in detail in the [pricing guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-b2b-case-study-cost/), so I'll keep it short.

Freelancers charge $100–$1,500. Quality varies *massively*. No video, no SEO, no structured data, no social, no CMS guidance. You're betting on the individual.

DIY costs $0–$500 and almost never ships. The case study goes on the to-do list, Q2 gets busy, the willing customer goes on holiday, and six months later it still hasn't happened. I hear this from nearly every company that reaches out to us.

## Every one of these makes you trade something

Here's the pattern once you've read the whole list.

The premium video shops want thousands a year and a month of your time. The writing specialists give you words on a page. No video, no social. The AI tools spit out a draft in a day that your buyer can smell was written by a machine. The freelancers are a coin-flip. And the big content agencies treat your case study as line seven on a rate card.

Every single one makes you give something up. Format, speed, price, authenticity, or focus. Pick your poison.

And if you try to *not* trade — to get every format, from real customers, done properly — you end up assembling it à la carte. Video from a video shop (~$3,000+). The written study from a writer ($1,750+). A video testimonial as a separate line ($2,000+). Then the testimonial pull-quote and the social. Add it up and you're at **$7,000–$10,000+**, across three or four vendors, on three or four timelines.

That's the trade I built Best Case Studio to refuse. One real conversation becomes your complete proof set (every format, everywhere your buyers look) for one fixed price of $2,500, in 14 days. That's the whole pitch.

## Get the most out of your case studies

Whichever option you ultimately go for, the whole exercise of case studies and social proof is to build trust. That's why it's so important to not let your case studies gather dust on your website.

You should be sending them out, posting versions of them on LinkedIn, referencing them from your home page and content.

Which is also why video is so useful. A case study video is essentially a well you can keep drawing content from. You can pull out several videos for social media, a testimonial video for the homepage, and have the full video on YouTube helping with SEO.

I've even seen some companies record a case study like a podcast, and release the whole thing on Spotify and other platforms. I'm not sure quite how effective that would be, but I like the thinking.

So don't see them as one and done. Treat them as evergreen content you can use all over the place.

Because the more your ICP sees this content, the more they'll trust you. And the more they trust you, the easier the sale.
